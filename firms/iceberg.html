Iceberg

Simulated Hyperliquid trading with an 80% split. Instant Funding or a 1-step evaluation, and USDC payouts from a public pool on Arbitrum.

Split: 80% Max Account: $100,000 Chain: Hyperliquid Token: No Rules Onchain: No Payouts verified: Not yet

Iceberg is a simulated-capital prop firm priced off live Hyperliquid markets. Orders do not hit the Hyperliquid book. Eligible funded profit is paid in real USDC from a public payout pool on Arbitrum, or taken as account credit. Purchases are non-refundable, and Iceberg says KYC is not required.

There are two products. Instant Funding starts funded, with no evaluation. 1-Step Unlimited starts in a one-step evaluation and opens a separate funded account after a 10% net profit target. Funded profit is split 80% to the trader and 20% to Iceberg on both.

Each person can hold up to $50,000 of Instant Funding at once, and up to $200,000 of funded 1-Step Unlimited allocation. A passed evaluation waits if that funded cap is full. Evaluation profit does not carry into the funded account and cannot be withdrawn.

Loss limits

Loss limits are checked against equity, so open P&L, trading fees, and funding count before a trade is closed. The daily floor is 97% of the UTC opening balance. The total floor stays at 94% of initial capital and does not trail a profit peak. Equity at or below either floor closes the account for good.

On a $25,000 account with an unchanged opening balance, the daily floor is $24,250 and the total floor is $23,500. News trading, overnight holds, and weekend holds are allowed. There is no minimum trading-day count, consistency score, or mandatory stop.

Copying trades between different users, account sharing, cross-account hedging, and exploiting delayed prices are prohibited. Opening the same trade across your own accounts is allowed.

Markets and leverage

The catalog lists 122 markets across crypto, equities, commodities, indices, and FX. The leverage cap is per market: up to 25× on EUR and JPY, 10× on BTC, ETH, SOL, and the major indices, 8× on gold and crude, and 2× on most other crypto. Position size and margin tiers can force a lower cap. Trading fees are the market’s base maker and taker rates, with no volume, staking, or referral discount. Hourly funding is separate.

Payouts

Only funded accounts can request a payout. Close positions and cancel orders first. Each Instant Funding request claims all eligible realized profit, capped at 5% of initial size. Profit above that cap is forfeited when the claim settles, and the balance resets to initial capital. A $25,000 Instant account pays the trader $200 on the $250 minimum claim and $1,000 on the $1,250 maximum.

1-Step Unlimited funded payouts have no cooldown and no 5% cap. Eligible gross profit must reach $25, and the trader receives 80%.

Wallet payouts are native USDC on Arbitrum One. Account credit can buy another account at 5% off and cannot be withdrawn as wallet USDC. Iceberg says accepted payouts are automated and built to arrive in seconds, without a later review that can revoke them. Payment still depends on available pool funds and can stay queued.

We have not independently verified Iceberg’s payouts. The pool address is public, so the balance and transfers can be checked on Arbiscan before treating a payout claim as settled.

Payout pool

Trading capital is simulated. The USDC that pays traders comes from account fees. Instant Funding fees send 99% to the pool and 1% to the protocol. A 1-Step purchase pays referral commissions and 3% to the protocol, and the rest goes to the pool (97% when there is no purchase commission).

Iceberg says it seeded the pool with a $30,000 insurance fund of its own capital, and that it has not raised venture funding. It also says that capital can be withdrawn as the pool builds its own reserve. When a payout would drop the pool under that reserve, the trader’s 80% is paid first and referral, insurance, and team shares are skipped until the reserve rebuilds.

On a payout, the trader receives 80%. A referrer’s commission comes out of Iceberg’s 20% and does not reduce the trader’s share. Of what remains, 15% is allocated to incentivize the insurance fund and 85% goes to the team.
