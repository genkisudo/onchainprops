A Rulebook Written to Be Audited, Payouts Not Yet Auditable, and an Invite Code to Get In

Northbook calls itself "the open-book prop firm" and its pitch is "funded trading you can audit". You pay a one-time fee, trade under two loss limits, pass by reaching a profit target and move to a funded account with on-demand USDC payouts. There is no time limit, no minimum trading days and no consistency rule, and the rulebook says that if a rule is not in the rulebook, it does not exist.

Important Context: What Northbook Is

Northbook is a new, invite-only beta. You need an invite code from a current user to open the app and buy a challenge, so the public pages are all you can read before getting in. We could not find a named operating company, a registered address or a licence on the site, and the footer says Northbook is "not licensed or regulated by any financial authority". We list the country as TBC.

It is not a fully onchain prop firm. The how-it-works page says challenge and funded accounts are matched internally (a B-book): fills, P&L and equity are simulated against Hyperliquid's live order book, and your orders never reach Hyperliquid's matching engine. The legal page says every account, funded ones included, is simulated, and that where Northbook mirrors a signal into a live market the position belongs to Northbook. That is why we mark Rules Onchain: No. What is meant to be onchain is the payout, which Northbook says settles in USDC by smart contract with a transaction hash, and that claim is the one we could not check yet (see Payout Verification below).

Account Types and Pricing

There are three one-step tracks in four sizes. All three share a 3% daily loss limit and the same split. They differ in target and in how much static drawdown you get. Prices below are the list prices struck through on the homepage on 27 November 2026; the cards showed about 10% less during the beta, and a BETA10 code was advertised.

Fees are one-time, paid in USDC and non-refundable once the account is activated. Base is the cheapest and leaves only 3% of room, because the daily limit and the drawdown floor start at the same number. Prime gives the most buffer at the highest price. Two optional extras are chosen at checkout: Split 90 raises the split from 80% to 90% for the life of the account, and Fee rebate returns 100% of the challenge fee with your first payout. The prices of both add-ons are not in the public rulebook.

The largest size on the homepage buttons is $50K, while the app's page description says "up to $200K". The rulebook caps a trader at $200,000 of combined active capital, challenge and funded accounts together, counted from the moment a challenge is bought. Any other split or size options that Northbook mentions outside the rulebook are not listed here, so we list the 80% base split and the 90% add-on only.

Drawdown and Loss Rules

Northbook uses a hard-breach model. Both limits are enforced on equity, which includes floating P&L on open positions, so you can breach without closing a trade. A momentary touch is a breach. On breach every open position is closed, the account is permanently disabled and no payout is available from it. There is no reset and no appeal, and you are not liable for losses beyond your fee.

The daily loss limit is 3% of your start-of-day balance, snapshotted at 00:00 UTC. The snapshot uses closed balance only, so floating profit does not raise the next day's floor, and banking profit grows the allowance while losses shrink it. The rulebook's own example shows an account breaching the daily limit on day three while still above its starting balance. The max drawdown is static: set once from the starting balance (a $50,000 account at 6% has a floor at $47,000) and never moved, even if equity reaches $60,000. There is no trailing drawdown on any Northbook product.

The profit target is also measured on equity, so an open winning trade counts toward it, and the challenge passes the moment equity reaches the target, with no review or waiting period. The funded account keeps the same starting balance and the same loss limits and drops the target.

Trading Rules, Leverage and Markets

Northbook lists 200+ markets priced against Hyperliquid's live order books: crypto perpetuals, single-stock perpetuals, index perpetuals and commodity perpetuals, with the homepage also showing forex pairs. Northbook chooses the tradeable list and can add or remove pairs. Leverage is capped per pair and never exceeds what Hyperliquid allows for that pair, with the current cap shown in the terminal, so we do not list a single maximum leverage figure.

Northbook says its simulated fees sit below Hyperliquid's standard schedule: 0.008% maker and 0.035% taker on native perps, and 0.0008% maker and 0.0035% taker on HIP-3 perps. Fees and funding count toward your profit and loss, so they also affect your limits. There is no monthly, inactivity, data or withdrawal fee.

What is allowed: scalping, swing, grid and mean-reversion strategies, news trading, weekend and overnight holding, no mandatory stop-loss, no profit cap, and copy trading including between your own Northbook accounts.

What is not:

  • High-frequency trading (order rates designed to exploit market microstructure, including quote stuffing and layering) and latency arbitrage or tick sniping. The rulebook publishes no numeric threshold for either.
  • Cross-account hedging, coordinated group trading, wash trading, account sharing or transfer, identity fraud, insider trading and exploiting venue or price-feed defects.
  • Account cycling: repeatedly buying challenges to take maximum-risk binary bets.

Breaking a conduct rule can mean a warning, termination without payout or a permanent ban. The FAQ says the account is flagged and you get a chance to respond first, but the rulebook also says every decision is made by the Northbook team, with automated monitoring only identifying activity for review.

Profit Split and Payouts

The split is 80% to you and 20% to Northbook, or 90% with the Split 90 add-on. The formula is (current balance minus starting balance) times your split. On the rulebook's worked example, a $100,000 funded account at $112,000 pays $9,600 to the trader and $2,400 to Northbook, and the balance resets to $100,000.

Payouts are on demand with no minimum waiting period and no limit on the number of requests. The minimum is $10, paid in USDC. All positions must be closed and all open orders cancelled first, each payout sweeps the full profit (no partial withdrawals), and the drawdown floor stays where it was.

Identity verification is not needed to buy a challenge or trade, but it is required before any payout: a government photo ID and a live selfie, with no proof of address. Northbook cannot serve people in or resident in comprehensive-sanctions jurisdictions.

The homepage sells payouts as "no queue, no discretion", yet the rulebook reserves the right to hold or reject a payout, or terminate an account, where it sees suspicious or prohibited activity. A rejection comes with a reason and locks the account for 14 days, during which you can dispute it by contacting support. That is a normal clause, but it means "instant" is a target, not a guarantee.

Payout Verification

Northbook says every payout is a USDC transaction with a hash you can open in a block explorer, and that a public onchain USDC vault is held at 15–20% or more of all funded capital. We could not check either claim. The rulebook does not say which chain payouts settle on, and neither the site nor the pages we read publish a payout wallet, a vault contract address, a sample transaction hash or an audited payout total. The "Transparency" and "API docs" links in the site footer were marked "Incoming".

We have not received a payout ourselves, and Northbook has no wallet in our automated payout tracking, so we mark Payouts Verified: No. This is "not yet verified", not "fails verification". We will upgrade the rating once there is a payout transaction we can reconcile against a published split on a block explorer, and a vault address we can read.

The beta also leaves open how many funded traders there are, how many have been paid and what the pass rate is. The app's own description promises a public pass rate, but we could not see one without an account.

What Gives Us Pause

Northbook's own disclaimer is unusually candid about its conflicts. It earns from challenge fees, including from traders who fail and buy again, and "when a signal is not mirrored into a live market we hold the other side of it, so we gain when that trade loses". It says its routing rules and the thresholds that decide which accounts get mirrored are published in the rulebook, but we found no numeric threshold on the pages we read. That is a structural B-book conflict, common in this industry and rarely stated this plainly.

On top of that: the operating company is not named and there is no licence, the legal page is a draft (v0.5 when we checked) and the product pages already disagree with each other on the largest account size and the split add-ons. Payout chain, vault address and pass rate are unpublished, access is by invite only, and we found no public track record of payouts to traders. The hard breach is unforgiving, and Base leaves a 3% floor. High-frequency and latency-arbitrage rules are enforced at Northbook's discretion without numbers.

The Verdict

Northbook has an unusually clear published rulebook: two limits, a static drawdown, no consistency rule, scalping and news trading allowed, and fees below Hyperliquid's own schedule. That makes it attractive for discretionary and scalping traders who find Bungee Funded Perps' scalping ban a deal breaker. But we put payout safety ahead of rule friendliness, and today the payout side is claims we cannot verify, from an unnamed operator that runs the other side of your trades. If you try it, start with a Base or Classic $5K or $10K account at about $25–$105, complete identity verification early, request a small first payout and check the transaction hash on a block explorer before scaling up. Re-read the live rulebook before you pay, because it is still a draft.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Northbook an onchain prop firm?

Only partly. Northbook says all accounts, funded ones included, are simulated and matched internally (a B-book) against Hyperliquid prices, so the rules are enforced by Northbook's own risk engine, not by smart contracts. What is onchain is the payout: Northbook says funded payouts settle in USDC by smart contract with a transaction hash, though it does not name the chain and publishes no payout wallet or vault address.

How do Northbook payouts work?

On a funded account you can request a payout at any time with no waiting period. The minimum is $10, all positions must be closed, and each payout sweeps the full profit above the starting balance at your split (80%, or 90% with the Split 90 add-on), after which the account resets to its starting balance. Identity verification (photo ID and a selfie) is required before the first payout, and Northbook reserves the right to hold or reject a payout and lock the account for 14 days.

What are the Northbook drawdown and daily loss rules?

Max drawdown is static: 3% on Base, 6% on Classic and 8% on Prime, measured from the starting balance and never moved by profit. The daily loss limit is 3% of the start-of-day balance, reset at 00:00 UTC. Both are enforced on equity including open positions, a momentary touch is a breach, and a breach permanently closes the account with no reset or appeal.

Does Northbook allow scalping, news trading and copy trading?

Yes to all three, with caveats. The rulebook says scalping, swing, grid and mean-reversion strategies, news trading, weekend and overnight holding, and copy trading (including between your own Northbook accounts) are permitted. It does ban high-frequency trading and latency arbitrage without publishing numeric thresholds, plus cross-account hedging, wash trading and coordinated group trading, and it has no consistency rule, minimum trading days or time limit.

How much does a Northbook challenge cost and can anyone join?

Fees are one-time, in USDC, and range from $25 (Base, $5K) to $770 (Prime, $50K) at list price. Northbook is in an invite-only beta, so you need an invite code from a current user to open the app and buy a challenge. Prices and access can change, so check the live site.
