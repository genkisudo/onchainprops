Listings are not endorsements, and this is not financial advice. Some outbound links on the site are affiliate links; the site states that this does not affect its rankings.

## What is an onchain prop firm?

An onchain prop trading firm provides capital to traders, typically in cryptocurrency. Traders usually pass an evaluation by trading on decentralized exchanges or onchain protocols rather than legacy brokers, then share profits with the firm. Many firms pay the trader’s share in stablecoins (such as USDC or USDT) to a non-custodial wallet.

“Onchain prop firm” is a loose label. It covers firms whose rules and payouts live in smart contracts, and firms that trade on a blockchain venue such as Hyperliquid but run everything else on their own servers. The label alone is not proof that payouts or rules can be verified.

## How reviews and payout verification work

Profit split and account size are easy to compare. Whether a firm actually pays is the part that costs traders money, and on a public chain it is something you can check. Onchain Props records, for each firm it can, whether **payouts have been verified** and whether **rules are enforced onchain**.

A **Yes** for payouts verified means payouts were found that could be checked (for example, with public transaction hashes). It is not a safety guarantee. A **No** does not mean a firm is a scam; it means payouts have not been verified yet, so you would be relying on trust. **Work in progress** means verification is incomplete. Statuses change — use the live table and the firm’s review page.

### Checklist before paying for a challenge

From the site’s guide, [Are Onchain Prop Firms Legit?](https://onchainprops.xyz/blog/are-onchain-prop-firms-legit):

1. **Find real payouts with transaction hashes.** A screenshot is not proof. A transaction hash you can open on a block explorer is.
2. **Check the treasury or reserve.** A visible onchain wallet or reserve shows whether there is money behind the promises.
3. **See where the rules live.** If payout eligibility is enforced by a smart contract, no one has to approve it by hand. If rules sit in the firm’s database, you are trusting the firm to apply them.
4. **Know which dashboard you are reading.** Onchain dashboards read from contracts and chain transactions. Offchain dashboards pull from the firm’s own database.
5. **Check how the firm makes money.** Does it earn when you win, or when you fail? See [A-book vs B-book](https://onchainprops.xyz/blog/how-do-prop-firms-make-money).
6. **Look at the team and the entity.** Named founders and a registered entity give you somewhere to go if a payout is refused.

No firm on the directory is described as passing all six perfectly, and nothing guarantees safety. The goal is to know what you are trusting before you pay. A short pre-purchase routine is also in that guide: pick three recent payouts, look each one up on a block explorer, check the treasury, read who approves payouts, and start with the smallest account size.

Red flags called out on that page include payout “proof” without transaction hashes, a ledger you cannot match to a chain, no visible treasury, vague “funded” account types, rules that can change quietly after you pay, and pressure to buy immediately.

## FAQ

### What is an onchain prop firm?

An onchain prop trading firm provides capital to traders, typically in cryptocurrency, and usually requires them to pass an evaluation by trading on decentralized exchanges or specific onchain protocols rather than legacy brokers. See the longer definition above, or the FAQ on [the homepage](https://onchainprops.xyz/#faq).

### Are onchain prop firms legit?

Some are, and some aren’t. The “onchain” label is not enough. Before you pay, verify payouts with transaction hashes, check whether a treasury or reserve is visible, and see whether rules are enforced by smart contracts. The fewer of these a firm can show, the more you are taking its word for it. Full checklist: [Are Onchain Prop Firms Legit?](https://onchainprops.xyz/blog/are-onchain-prop-firms-legit).

### How do I verify a prop firm payout?

Ask for payout transaction hashes and look them up on a block explorer. Confirm the amount, the date, and the receiving wallet. If the firm publishes a treasury address, compare its balance to outstanding obligations. Regular stablecoin transfers to many distinct wallets are a stronger signal than screenshots or testimonials. Complement that with the usual checks: audit status, community feedback, and whether the team is publicly known. Most onchain prop firms are not regulated like traditional brokers, so due diligence is on you.

### What is A-booking vs B-booking?

**A-booking** means orders are routed to the real exchange — for example, Hyperliquid’s live order books. The firm does not take the other side of your trade. Profits and losses come from live market conditions, and the firm earns through fees and spread rather than from trader losses.

**B-booking** means orders are executed against a simulated environment that mirrors real market prices. The firm acts as the counterparty. The experience can look identical to live trading, but no orders actually touch the market. A longer explanation is in [How Do Crypto Prop Firms Make Money?](https://onchainprops.xyz/blog/how-do-prop-firms-make-money).

### How do crypto payouts work on these firms?

Most onchain prop firms in the directory pay to a non-custodial wallet in stablecoins such as USDC or USDT, which leaves a public onchain record. Methods and networks vary by firm, and not every firm pays this way. Always check a firm’s payout method on its review page before signing up if self-custody settlement matters to you.

## About this repo

This repo is a **static site**: vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, deployed to **GitHub Pages** from the `main` branch (custom domain: `onchainprops.xyz`). There is no app server.

**Single source of truth.** Firm facts (splits, account sizes, chains, drawdowns, payout verification) live in `firms.json`. FAQ copy lives in `faq.json`. Blog posts are written as Markdown in `posts/` and built into `blog/`. A zero-dependency build script regenerates derived copies — including table data, some `index.html` regions, `llms.txt`, and the sitemap:

```bash
node build.js
```

A GitHub Actions workflow runs the same script on every push to `main` and commits the result if files changed. To add or update a firm, edit `firms.json` only — not the generated regions marked `GEN:BEGIN` / `GEN:END`.

**Run locally:**

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open [http://localhost:8000](http://localhost:8000).
